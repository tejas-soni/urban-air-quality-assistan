Urban winter traffic emissions and shallow morning inversions combine to produce sharp `PM2.5`, black carbon (`BC`), and `NO2` concentration peaks during the `07:30–09:00` commute surge. Mobile monitoring studies across congested corridors show ambient concentrations begin receding as early morning traffic thins and solar heating weakens the surface inversion.

Shifting departure by `30–60 minutes` later (e.g., `09:15` instead of `08:15` where work or hybrid schedules allow) avoids peak stop-and-go congestion and cuts inhaled commuter `PM2.5` dose significantly.

## Key Action & Quantified Impact
- Shift morning departure `30–60 minutes` later after peak rush hour to lower commute `PM2.5` exposure by ~15–25% and cut idling fuel waste.
