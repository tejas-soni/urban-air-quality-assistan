Dry winter smog irritates nasal passages and slows mucociliary clearance — the tiny hair-like cilia that sweep trapped dust out of upper airways. Drinking adequate water and using sterile isotonic saline nasal sprays can ease upper-airway dryness after unavoidable commuting.

Supportive hydration does **not** filter fine `PM2.5` from entering deep lung alveoli; always pair comfort habits with primary exposure reduction (N95 masks outdoors, HEPA filtration indoors).

## Key Action & Quantified Impact
- Combine upper-airway hydration with an `N95` respirator outdoors (`>=95%` filtration) and clean indoor air (`<=15 µg/m³`) for real exposure reduction.
