Urban smog contains coarse dust (`PM10`), traffic-related nitrogen dioxide (`NO2`), and ground-level ozone (`O3`). The WHO 2021 Air Quality Guidelines establish 24-hour safety thresholds of `45 µg/m³` for `PM10`, `25 µg/m³` for `NO2`, and an 8-hour daily maximum of `100 µg/m³` for `O3`.

In winter, `NO2` and `PM10` spike near arterial roads during rush hours, whereas `O3` typically rises on sunny afternoons or in warmer shoulder months.

## Key Action & Quantified Impact
- Track multi-pollutant readings rather than `PM2.5` alone: avoiding high-`NO2` curbside corridors reduces airway inflammation risk where `NO2` exceeds `25 µg/m³`.
